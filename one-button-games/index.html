One-button games

Easy to learn, hard to master, and playable anywhere with one finger.

A one-button game gives you a single input, a tap or a hold, and builds all the challenge around when you use it. With nothing to learn, all of the depth goes into timing.

What makes a good one-button game

  • Readable patterns: you lose because you mistimed, never because you could not see.
  • Instant restarts: a failed run costs a second, not a menu.
  • Small, steady escalation: one new idea at a time, so every level feels learnable.
  • Works everywhere: a mouse button, a space bar and a fingertip should all feel the same.

Hold versus tap

Tap games, like Flappy Bird, ask for precise single moments. Hold games, like Void Bounce, ask you to choose how long to act, which turns waiting into a skill. Holding to roll and releasing to stop lets you pause and study the next obstacle, which is why Void Bounce can build 850 levels on a single button.

One-button games to play online

  • Void Bounce: hold to roll a bouncing ball through a neon tunnel. 850 levels, free in your browser. Play now.
  • Flappy Bird-style games: tap to flap through pipes. The purest test of single-tap timing.
  • Geometry Dash: tap to jump in time with the music across spikes and platforms.
  • Stack and tower games: tap to drop a block at the right moment and build as high as you can.
  • Helix Jump-style games: one input turns a tower to drop a ball through gaps. See games like Helix Jump.
